World ramp

The island's squares, then the first 40 goals of the generated world, as a player who plays each goal's proof gets them: before the ramp (each goal the best the search finds near the last) and after it (the same, but at most a capped number of fewest moves, rising from 8 to 20 over the first 30 goals, then no cap). Seeds: .

What it shows

  • Before the ramp the world is flat from its first goal: about 10 fewest moves at the median, spikes to over 20, right after the island's last square (15).
  • The cap trims the spikes early on, but the median rises little (8 for goals 1-10, about 10 by goal 30): the search ranks goals by difficulty for their distance, so it seldom picks one near the cap. It is a gentler start, not a climb.
  • Past goal 30 (no cap) both are alike, as they should be.
  • The game's quick measure is nearly always right: the long one found fewer moves than the goal's proof for 4-7% of goals, 0.1-0.2 moves on average.

The island

Fewest moves are exact, for all of a square's goals together (a breadth-first search over every board; a goal counts as its block lands on its target). The last square's include the blocker past its bay and the way on to its target. Distance: cells the red block goes, to its bay (the last: its target).

Notes

  • Fewest moves count one block's slide, however far, as one move.
  • The simulated player plays each goal's proof, so the board goes on as the game's own solutions leave it; real players leave it otherwise.
  • The world's first goal is made while you wait (effort 0) in both runs; in the game it is now made while the island is played (effort 3).

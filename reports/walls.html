Walls and repeated moves

Does a good goal need blocks moved more than once, and do walls (fixed cells) in the generated world make that more common? Fresh boards swept over the share of cells that are walls × the block density (the layout's tries per 4×4 chunk; the game's is 12), today's maker asked for a goal round the origin as for the world's first, at effort , no ramp aim. Every number comes from the maker's own proof: no other search for a shorter one.

What it shows

  • The gut feeling holds for long goals and not for walls. Blocks moved twice or more come with long proofs: in about 90% of proofs over 15 moves, a third of those of 11-15, under 10% of shorter ones, whatever the walls. Block density is what makes proofs long: at no walls the share with a re-moved block goes from 4% (density 6) to 64% (16).
  • Random walls make re-moved blocks rarer, not more common: from 35% of goals with none to 10% at 15% walls (all densities), a directed blocker cycle from 10% to 4%. The goals the maker finds get shorter as walls go up (12.6 moves at the mean with none, 6.7 at 15%), with fewer decisions and a shorter run for red. Its push search stops at a wall, so it finds shallower goals, and walls take cells from blocks (at 15% walls a density-16 board has the block fill of density 8 without).
  • At the same proof length walls do not lower the share; if anything they raise it a little (proofs of 7-10 moves: 4% with none, 7-9% from 6% walls; at effort 2, proofs of 11-15: 6% to 17-23%, few goals). So a wall makes a goal of a given length more order-bound, but today's maker trades that for shorter goals.
  • Effort 2 (more candidates, wider measures; 60 boards a cell at 4 wall shares × densities 8, 12, 16) shows the same trend with walls, so it is not effort 0's narrow search alone.
  • Every board gave a legal goal, at every wall share and density.
  • Up to about 4% walls little changes at the game's density (moves, re-moved blocks, found rate as without): the game's walls slider starts there.
  • Smarter wall placement (walls that make dead ends red has to plan round, rather than walls anywhere) and a maker that looks for long goals on walled boards are what could make walls raise it; the Judge does not value re-moved blocks today.

The derived numbers

  • Repeated block: a block moves 2 or more times in the proof. Exact for the proof; another shortest solution might move blocks fewer times (not searched).
  • Back-and-forth: the proof returns to a block it already left (with back-to-back slides of one block merged, every repeat is a return); reversal: a block slides back the way it came at some point. Both from the proof alone.
  • Blocker graph (approximation): an edge A → B when B's slide sweeps a cell A was the last block to leave, from the proof's order alone (not from which blocks truly had to move: a block that left a cell for some other reason still counts). Cyclomatic complexity = edges − nodes + components over the blocks moved, directed edges counted once each (A → B and B → A both count, so a pair that must take turns gives 1). Directed cycle: some block is, through the chain, in its own way.
  • Detour: the proof's moves per cell of red's straight distance (start to target), median.
  • Fewest moves is the proof's length (the Judge's moves term with no Auditor): the maker's window measure finds it exact for the share shown as "exact"; elsewhere it is an upper bound.
  • Decisions and fitness are the Judge's (src/judge.ts) on the proof's length; maker time is the median of one process each, on the VM (2 processes on 2 cores).

# Papers: annotated bibliography

What the literature gives Infinite Unblock Me, grouped by theme. ★ marks the most relevant. Each entry
links its cleaned markdown (`cleaned/<slug>/<slug>.md`: full text, MathJax, tables, described figures,
front matter with citation, licence and a relevance note). Originals are in `raw/papers/`; citations,
URLs and licences in [raw/FINDS.md](raw/FINDS.md). Copyrighted conversions are on the VM only (see
[README](README.md)).

"Chat" and "notes" mean the user's two AI-written texts
([context/ai-chat-state-space-and-difficulty.txt](context/ai-chat-state-space-and-difficulty.txt),
[context/ai-research-notes.md](context/ai-research-notes.md)); their claims are checked in
[context/ai-notes-verification.md](context/ai-notes-verification.md). Corrections are flagged ⚠ below.

Before building on any of this, read the notes' section 12 ("Open research questions") and 14
("Evidence standards") in [context/ai-research-notes.md](context/ai-research-notes.md), and remember that
the game's own measures (extra moves, decisions, mixedness) were tested on one player's few verdicts
([reports.md](reports.md)).

## Start here: the ten most useful

1. ★ [olieslagers2026](cleaned/olieslagers2026-backward-reasoning-rush-hour/olieslagers2026-backward-reasoning-rush-hour.md): AND-OR subgoal trees for Rush Hour and a one-parameter player model; 80–90% of human moves are "sensible"; code and human move logs available.
2. ★ [fogleman2018 blog](cleaned/fogleman2018-solving-rush-hour-blog/fogleman2018-solving-rush-hour-blog.md) + `raw/data/fogleman-rush/`: every interesting 6×6 puzzle (2.58 M) with optimal length and cluster size; the dataset to calibrate any difficulty number against.
3. ★ [jarusek2011](cleaned/jarusek2011-transport-puzzle-difficulty/jarusek2011-transport-puzzle-difficulty.md): for Rush Hour, optimal length already predicts human solving time (Pearson 0.77, Spearman 0.90); their simulated player adds nothing. The baseline any new measure must beat.
4. ★ [fernau2003](cleaned/fernau2003-parameterized-complexity-generalized-rush-hour/fernau2003-parameterized-complexity-generalized-rush-hour.md): Rush Hour on the infinite plane; finitely many "event" stopping positions per car suffice (bounded by car count, not board size), with the exact candidate set for bounded move counts. The formal basis for the endless board.
5. ★ [bennati2014](cleaned/bennati2014-gestalt-rush-hour/bennati2014-gestalt-rush-hour.md): aligned parallel cars read as one object; such "distracting clusters" cut optimal solutions from 77% to 31% without changing solvability. A visual difficulty lever outside the state graph.
6. ★ [vanassema2014](cleaned/vanassema2014-hardness-6x6-rush-hour/vanassema2014-hardness-6x6-rush-hour.md): full 6×6 enumeration under our move model (max 51 moves; hardness falls ~×1.44 per extra move); argues length alone isn't difficulty.
7. ★ [delsolar2026](cleaned/delsolar2026-safari-rush-hour-state-representations/delsolar2026-safari-rush-hour-state-representations.md): per-line ranking, move-closed clusters, retrograde analysis per cluster, hill climbing over clusters to find long puzzles.
8. ★ [ho2022](cleaned/ho2022-simplified-mental-representations/ho2022-simplified-mental-representations.md) and [correa2023](cleaned/correa2023-humans-decompose-tasks/correa2023-humans-decompose-tasks.md): people plan in simplified worlds (attend only to obstacles that change the plan) and pick subgoals that save search; matches the user's latest hunch that difficulty comes from which blocks *look* relevant.
9. ★ [korf2002](cleaned/korf2002-disjoint-pattern-databases/korf2002-disjoint-pattern-databases.md) / [felner2004](cleaned/felner2004-additive-pattern-databases/felner2004-additive-pattern-databases.md): additive lower bounds; since one block moves per move, per-group tables that count only their own moves can be summed.
10. ★ [wehrle2014](cleaned/wehrle2014-efficient-stubborn-sets/wehrle2014-efficient-stubborn-sets.md) (+ [alkhazraji2012](cleaned/alkhazraji2012-stubborn-sets-optimal-planning/alkhazraji2012-stubborn-sets-optimal-planning.md), [roger2020](cleaned/roger2020-atom-centric-stubborn-sets/roger2020-atom-centric-stubborn-sets.md)): strong stubborn sets, the safe, optimality-preserving move-order pruning; Fast Downward has the code.

## 1. Rush Hour and sliding blocks

- ★ **olieslagers2026**: Olieslagers, Bnaya, Lin, Ma. A computational model of backward reasoning in human problem solving. *Computational Brain & Behavior* 2026. CC BY. [md](cleaned/olieslagers2026-backward-reasoning-rush-hour/olieslagers2026-backward-reasoning-rush-hour.md). OR node "get car X out of the way" → AND node "a move of X" → children = cars blocking it; cycles cut; leaves are sensible moves. Player stops at each OR node with probability γ, so a leaf at depth d gets weight $(1-\gamma)^d$ (closed form). Humans prefer shallow leaves; RT grows with depth; far from the goal only half the sensible moves are optimal. Beats forward search, Eureka, hill climbing. 70 puzzles of 9 cars, optimal length 5–14. Code `raw/code/rush_hour`, data `raw/data/osf-23vrb`. ⚠ It models the next move, not puzzle difficulty; no per-puzzle score. Paper inconsistency on λ vs 1−λ (noted in file).
- ★ **bennati2014**: Bennati, Brüssow, Ragni, Konieczny. Gestalt effects in planning: Rush Hour as an example. CogSci 2014. [md](cleaned/bennati2014-gestalt-rush-hour/bennati2014-gestalt-rush-hour.md). 30 people, 24 boards, eye tracking. Clusters with both ends aligned hurt optimality most. Also names the "longest move" shortcut. ⚠ Chat's "optimal among correct" label is wrong: both percentages are of all trials.
- ★ **delsolar2026**: del Solar-Zavala, Buro, Barriga. Effective state representations and search for interesting Safari Rush Hour instances. *IEEE Access* 14, 2026. CC BY. [md](cleaned/delsolar2026-safari-rush-hour-state-representations/delsolar2026-safari-rush-hour-state-representations.md). Clusters (same arrangement type per line) are closed under moves; retrograde over a cluster ~3× faster than IDA* per board; cluster size vs longest solution Spearman 0.72; best 131 moves; 11–14 pieces on 7×7 is the sweet spot. ⚠ It's peer-reviewed, not a "July 2026 preprint"; authors call the 0.72 an optimistic upper bound.
- ★ **vanassema2014**: van Assema. Hardness of 6×6 Rush Hour. BSc thesis, U. Amsterdam 2014. [md](cleaned/vanassema2014-hardness-6x6-rush-hour/vanassema2014-hardness-6x6-rush-hour.md). 40.1e9 legal / 31.5e9 solvable configurations, max 51 slides, all ≥40-move boards have 10–14 vehicles. "Cluster groups" by line fillings predate delsolar2026. Shows two 31-move boards with very different graphs.
- **collette2006**: Collette, Raskin, Servais. Symbolic computation of the hardest Rush Hour configurations. Computers and Games 2006. [md](cleaned/collette2006-hardest-rush-hour-configurations/collette2006-hardest-rush-hour-configurations.md). BDD backward search over all 6×6 boards; per-line encoding beats per-car; "non-dominated" hard starts. ⚠ Its 93 counts single-square steps; in slides the hardest 6×6 needs 51 (vanassema2014, Fogleman). The chat's ScienceDirect link for it doesn't exist.
- **trenquelleon2023**: de Batz de Trenquelléon, Choukarah, Roucairol, Addoum, Cazenave. Procedural generation of Rush Hour levels. CG 2022 (LNCS 2023). [md](cleaned/trenquelleon2023-procedural-generation-rush-hour/trenquelleon2023-procedural-generation-rush-hour.md). Blocking-car closure heuristic for greedy best-first; CNN solvability classifier (~100% 6×6, 88.6% 7×7). ⚠ The 87.5% difficulty accuracy doesn't match its own confusion matrix (85.3%), and hard classes are never predicted. Difficulty = optimal length only.
- **hauptman2009**: Hauptman, Elyasaf, Sipper, Karmon. GP-Rush. GECCO 2009. [md](cleaned/hauptman2009-gp-rush/hauptman2009-gp-rush.md). Recursive blocker lower bound; evolved rule policies cut IDA* nodes to 40% (6×6) and 10% (8×8); relaxing a level by removing cars can make it trivial, so naive PDBs are weak.
- **cian2022**: Cian, Dreossi, Dovier. Modeling and solving Rush Hour (MiniZinc, ASP). CILC 2022. CC BY. [md](cleaned/cian2022-modeling-solving-rush-hour/cian2022-modeling-solving-rush-hour.md). Baseline only; MiniZinc+Chuffed solves 35 Fogleman puzzles in ~2 s. (No Prolog/PDDL, despite FINDS' first note.)
- ★ **fogleman2018**: Fogleman. Solving Rush Hour, the puzzle (blog, July 2018). [md](cleaned/fogleman2018-solving-rush-hour-blog/fogleman2018-solving-rush-hour-blog.md). Bitboard solver, full enumeration, clustering; database of 2,577,412 puzzles (no-wall: 476,118; peak 11 moves; max 51). Code `raw/code/rush` (MIT).

## 2. Complexity and infinite boards

- ★ **fernau2003**: Fernau, Hagerup, Nishimura, Ragde, Reinhardt. Parameterized complexity of a generalized Rush Hour puzzle. CCCG 2003. [md](cleaned/fernau2003-parameterized-complexity-generalized-rush-hour/fernau2003-parameterized-complexity-generalized-rush-hour.md). Real-valued positions on the plane; each of k cars needs at most $2k^3$ discretized stops (solutions at most k times longer); for bounded move count the candidate set (start, goal, lane borders, chained car-length offsets) keeps the count exact. ⚠ Answers the notes' open question 1 for solvability: the "critical-position abstraction" the AI proposed as new is already proven, but needs chained offsets, not just collision endpoints.
- **brumleve2012**: Brumleve, Hamkins, Schlicht. The mate-in-n problem of infinite chess is decidable. CiE 2012. [md](cleaned/brumleve2012-infinite-chess-mate-in-n-decidable/brumleve2012-infinite-chess-mate-in-n-decidable.md). Infinitely many moves per position, still decidable at fixed depth via Presburger arithmetic; "far enough moves are all alike". Applies to "solvable in ≤ n slides" with finitely many blocks; no practical bound.
- **evans2014**: Evans, Hamkins. Transfinite game values in infinite chess. *Integers* 2014. [md](cleaned/evans2014-transfinite-game-values-infinite-chess/evans2014-transfinite-game-values-infinite-chess.md). Ordinal values need an adversary; a one-player puzzle always has a finite optimal length. Mostly background.
- **geller2022**: Geller. Baba Is You is undecidable. arXiv 2022. [md](cleaned/geller2022-baba-is-you-undecidable/geller2022-baba-is-you-undecidable.md). Undecidability needs an unbounded hallway plus rule rewriting, self-movers, copying. Lesson: avoid mechanics that write unbounded strings.
- **hearn2006**: Hearn. Games, Puzzles, and Computation. MIT PhD thesis 2006. [md](cleaned/hearn2006-games-puzzles-computation-thesis/hearn2006-games-puzzles-computation-thesis.md). Nondeterministic Constraint Logic (AND, protected OR, latch); Rush Hour PSPACE-complete with 1×2/1×3 cars (gadgets transcribed); infinite boards only an open question. Vocabulary for designing dependency chains.
- **hearn2005**, **tromp2005** (Limits of Rush Hour logic complexity), **brunner2020** (1×1 Rush Hour with fixed blocks is PSPACE-complete), **brunner2025** (Push-1 without fixed walls): complexity of variants; see cleaned folders `hearn2005-sliding-block-ncl`, `tromp2005-limits-rush-hour-logic`, `brunner2020-1x1-rush-hour-fixed-blocks`, `brunner2025-push-1-without-fixed-walls-pspace`.

## 3. Difficulty measures and human models

- ★ **jarusek2011**: Jarušek, Pelánek. What determines difficulty of transport puzzles? FLAIRS 2011. [md](cleaned/jarusek2011-transport-puzzle-difficulty/jarusek2011-transport-puzzle-difficulty.md). 45 Rush Hour levels; random walk with bonus B=25 for distance-reducing moves. ⚠ The chat says state-space structure explains Rush Hour difficulty beyond length; for Rush Hour it doesn't (the model helps only where moves can't be undone).
- **jarusek2010**: Jarušek, Pelánek. Difficulty rating of Sokoban puzzle. STAIRS 2010. [md](cleaned/jarusek2010-sokoban-difficulty-rating/jarusek2010-sokoban-difficulty-rating.md). Best metric: fewest switches between two box groups along a solution (Spearman 0.82); state-space size useless. Close cousin of the game's "mixed come-backs".
- **pelanek2014**: Pelánek. Difficulty rating of Sudoku puzzles. arXiv 2014. [md](cleaned/pelanek2014-sudoku-difficulty-rating/pelanek2014-sudoku-difficulty-rating.md). Simulated greedy human (simplest technique first): step difficulty + "dependency" (available next moves) reach r=0.88; search-effort metrics did poorly.
- ★ **chen2023**: Chen, White, Sturtevant. Entropy as a measure of puzzle difficulty. AIIDE 2023. [md](cleaned/chen2023-entropy-puzzle-difficulty/chen2023-entropy-puzzle-difficulty.md). Recursive entropy over "sensible" moves defined by player-knowledge rules; ReMUSE r=0.57 with Witness ratings vs 0.47 for solution length. Exhaustive, small boards only.
- ★ **shen2024**: Shen, Sturtevant. Generalized entropy and solution information. AIIDE 2024. [md](cleaned/shen2024-generalized-entropy/shen2024-generalized-entropy.md). Total solution information $-\log_2$ P(policy reaches a goal): computable by rollouts or DP over any player model; "hard unless you know the trick" = big gap between two player models. Witness only.
- **schaa2026**: Schaa, Quiroz, Barriga. Modeling puzzle difficulty via A* search behavior. Preprint 2026. [md](cleaned/schaa2026-astar-search-behavior-difficulty/schaa2026-astar-search-behavior-difficulty.md). On 42 Safari Rush Hour levels, plain solution length ρ≈0.96; lower branching = harder. ⚠ Datasets analysed separately; structural metrics only for Sokoban.
- **valenzuela2025**: Valenzuela, Schaa, Barriga, Patow. Search statistics for maze and puzzle difficulty. *Entertainment Computing* 2025, CC BY-NC (printed on p. 1). [md](cleaned/valenzuela2025-search-statistics-difficulty/valenzuela2025-search-statistics-difficulty.md). Sokoban: A* closed list vs perceived difficulty only 0.286; long levels read as tedious.
- **kristensen2024**: Kristensen, Burelli. Difficulty modelling in mobile puzzle games. *IJCGT* 2024. CC BY. [md](cleaned/kristensen2024-difficulty-mobile-puzzle-games/kristensen2024-difficulty-mobile-puzzle-games.md). Agent-simulation features beat static level features 5–6× for unreleased levels. ⚠ One game; agent data helped only cold-start/cohort predictions.
- **kristensen2020** (RL agent's best 5% runs rank levels by completion rate, ρ≈−0.81) and **kristensen2021** (moves-per-attempt as a negative binomial; ~2 points completion per move of limit): [2020](cleaned/kristensen2020-completion-rate-reinforcement-learning/kristensen2020-completion-rate-reinforcement-learning.md), [2021](cleaned/kristensen2021-statistical-modelling-level-difficulty/kristensen2021-statistical-modelling-level-difficulty.md). Useful once there are many players.
- **vankreveld2015**: van Kreveld, Löffler, Mutser. Automated puzzle difficulty estimation. CIG 2015. [md](cleaned/vankreveld2015-automated-puzzle-difficulty-estimation/vankreveld2015-automated-puzzle-difficulty-estimation.md). For "Move" (sliding, closest to us) counterintuitive moves (r=0.45) and minimum moves dominate; board size and piece count barely matter.
- **chu2025**: Chu, Zheng, Fan. What makes people think a puzzle is fun to solve? CogSci 2025. CC BY. [md](cleaned/chu2025-what-makes-puzzle-fun/chu2025-what-makes-puzzle-fun.md). Fun ≠ difficulty (enjoyment vs rated difficulty r=−0.95 in the lab); liked levels have few boxes with long per-box paths. Code/data `raw/code/fun-puzzles_cogsci25`.
- ★ **ho2022**: Ho et al. People construct simplified mental representations to plan. *Nature* 2022. [md](cleaned/ho2022-simplified-mental-representations/ho2022-simplified-mental-representations.md). Value-guided construal: attention goes to obstacles that change the plan, even far away; nearby irrelevant ones are ignored. Suggests an "attention load" measure and a definition of decoys (our extrapolation).
- ★ **correa2023**: Correa et al. Humans decompose tasks by trading off utility and computational cost. *PLOS CB* 2023. CC BY. [md](cleaned/correa2023-humans-decompose-tasks/correa2023-humans-decompose-tasks.md). Chosen subgoals ≈ high betweenness centrality in the state graph; "search saved by the best subgoal" as a cheap decomposability score.
- **vanopheusden2023**: van Opheusden et al. Expertise increases planning depth in human gameplay. *Nature* 2023. [md](cleaned/vanopheusden2023-expertise-planning-depth/vanopheusden2023-expertise-planning-depth.md). Fitted best-first player with pruning, stop probability, feature dropout, lapses; predicts 41% of moves. Template for a simulated player.
- **huys2012**: Huys et al. Bonsai trees in your head. *PLOS CB* 2012. CC BY. [md](cleaned/huys2012-bonsai-trees-pruning/huys2012-bonsai-trees-pruning.md). People stop lookahead after a large loss deeper in the tree: solutions that need a bad-looking move a few steps in are missed. Fits "counterintuitive moves".

## 4. Generation

- **kartal2016**: Kartal, Sohre, Guy. Data-driven Sokoban generation with MCTS. AIIDE 2016. [md](cleaned/kartal2016-sokoban-mcts-generation/kartal2016-sokoban-mcts-generation.md). Build board then simulate play; solvable by construction; score r²=0.91 with perceived difficulty. ⚠ Weights set by hand; validation had 6 people. Code `raw/code/sokoban-level-generator-mcts` (no licence; contains a .dll, don't run).
- **bento2019**: Bento, Pereira, Lelis. Procedural generation of initial states of Sokoban. IJCAI 2019. [md](cleaned/bento2019-sokoban-initial-states/bento2019-sokoban-initial-states.md). Backward search from goals ordered by novelty then PDB "conflicts" (interaction between k pieces). ⚠ "Harder than human-designed" only for the aggregate of 20 runs.
- **taylor2011**: Taylor, Parberry. Procedural generation of Sokoban levels. GAMEON-NA 2011. [md](cleaned/taylor2011-procedural-generation-sokoban/taylor2011-procedural-generation-sokoban.md). Templates + reverse search to the farthest state; "box lines" track difficulty better than moves.
- **sturtevant2013**: Sturtevant. Large-scale BFS for game design: Fling!. AIIDE 2013 workshop. [md](cleaned/sturtevant2013-large-scale-bfs-fling/sturtevant2013-large-scale-bfs-fling.md). Retrograde database of all solvable boards; tests whether adding/removing a piece changes solvability. ⚠ Workshop paper; explicitly not automatic generation.
- **sturtevant2018**: Sturtevant, Ota. Exhaustive procedural content generation. AIIDE 2018. [md](cleaned/sturtevant2018-exhaustive-pcg/sturtevant2018-exhaustive-pcg.md). Rank/unrank of placements, retrograde bit tables, quality = brute-force tree / expert tree.
- **smith2010**: Smith, Whitehead. Analyzing the expressive range of a level generator. PCG 2010. [md](cleaned/smith2010-expressive-range-level-generator/smith2010-expressive-range-level-generator.md). Plot 10,000 generated levels on two metrics to see the generator's spread and bias.
- **alvarez2019**: Alvarez, Dahlskog, Font, Togelius. Interactive constrained MAP-Elites. CoG 2019. [md](cleaned/alvarez2019-constrained-map-elites/alvarez2019-constrained-map-elites.md). Archive of best level per feature cell: varied hard levels instead of one hardest. Qualitative only.
- **dart2012**: Dart, Nelson. Smart terrain causality chains (web note of the CIG 2012 paper). [md](cleaned/dart2012-smart-terrain-causality-chains/dart2012-smart-terrain-causality-chains.md). Back-chain from the objective through cause/effect rules, place only what the chain needs; more parallel chains = easier.

## 5. State-space reduction (solver side)

- ★ **alkhazraji2012**, **wehrle2012**, **wehrle2014**, **roger2020**: strong stubborn sets. Original definition and A* optimality (alkhazraji); theory map incl. sleep sets for tree search (wehrle2012); generalized SSS, static goal choice, mutex interference (wehrle2014); atom-centric computation without the action-pair table (roger2020). ⚠ The chat's pseudocode ("add every *enabled* interfering action") is unsound; the published rule adds all interfering actions. ⚠ Atom-centric returns the same sets, it doesn't skip action enumeration.
- **wehrle2015**: POR + symmetry elimination for cost-optimal planning. IJCAI 2015. [md](cleaned/wehrle2015-por-symmetry-elimination/wehrle2015-por-symmetry-elimination.md). Safe combination; one representative per symmetric move class (identical blocks).
- **pochter2011**: Problem symmetries in state-based planners. AAAI 2011. [md](cleaned/pochter2011-problem-symmetries-state-based-planners/pochter2011-problem-symmetries-state-based-planners.md). Automorphisms fixing start and goal; doesn't exploit translations.
- **gnad2016** / **gnad2018** / **gnad2019**: decoupled search and its combination with SSS. [2016](cleaned/gnad2016-decoupled-strong-stubborn-sets/gnad2016-decoupled-strong-stubborn-sets.md), [2018 star topology](cleaned/gnad2018-star-topology-decoupled-search/gnad2018-star-topology-decoupled-search.md), [2019 JAIR](cleaned/gnad2019-strong-stubborn-sets-decoupled-search/gnad2019-strong-stubborn-sets-decoupled-search.md). ⚠ Leaves may not interact at all: colliding blocks must be in the center, so it fits Unblock Me poorly. ⚠ The 2016 fork definition is wrong (fixed in 2019). Exponential gains are theoretical; benchmark gains small; use a "safety belt" that turns pruning off.
- **goubault2013**: A geometric view of partial order reduction. ENTCS 2013 (CC BY-NC-ND). [md](cleaned/goubault2013-geometric-view-por/goubault2013-geometric-view-por.md). Vocabulary for "commuting squares, holes, distinct solution classes". ⚠ That picture is only the motivation; the result is persistent sets vs future components in acyclic systems.
- **keren2018**: Strong stubborn sets for goal recognition design. ICAPS 2018. [md](cleaned/keren2018-stubborn-sets-goal-recognition-design/keren2018-stubborn-sets-goal-recognition-design.md). Safe pruning over *level edits*; needs independent, monotone edits (difficulty rarely is).
- **korf2002** / **felner2004**: (additive) pattern databases, dynamic partitioning. ⚠ Chat says adding PDBs needs cost partitioning; for one-piece-per-move puzzles disjoint groups counting their own moves suffice.
- **seipp2018** (CEGAR Cartesian abstractions) and **seipp2020** (saturated cost partitioning): background for abstraction heuristics; folders `seipp2018-cegar-cartesian-abstraction`, `seipp2020-saturated-cost-partitioning`.
- **junghanns2001**: Sokoban relevance cuts. TCS 2001. [md](cleaned/junghanns2001-sokoban-relevance-cuts/junghanns2001-sokoban-relevance-cuts.md). Cut moves far from recent ones: ~5× fewer nodes but unsafe (6 of 44 non-optimal). Keeps a big-board solver local.
- **fastdownward-pruning-methods-docs** and **prob-state-space-visualization** (web): [Fast Downward options](cleaned/fastdownward-pruning-methods-docs/fastdownward-pruning-methods-docs.md) (incl. `limited_pruning`), [ProB](cleaned/prob-state-space-visualization/prob-state-space-visualization.md) (Rush Hour full state graph 4782 states; POR picture 5410→208 is a threads model, not Rush Hour). Code: `raw/code/downward`.

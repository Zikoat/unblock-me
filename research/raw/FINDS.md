# Research finds (raw)

Collected 2026-11-11. Everything here is untrusted third-party material: code and data were cloned/downloaded only, never built or run.
Papers live in `papers/<slug>.pdf` (title page checked with pymupdf), web copies in `web/<slug>/` (page.html, page.md via html2text, SOURCE.txt), code in `code/<repo>/` (shallow clones), data in `data/<slug>/`.

License shorthand: **CC-BY** etc. = may be redistributed under that licence; **arXiv** = arXiv non-exclusive licence (link, don't re-host unless the PDF says CC); **author copy** = free copy on an author/institution site, publisher holds copyright; **© AAAI/IJCAI/ACM/IEEE/Springer/Elsevier** = free to read, not to re-publish. Only CC-licensed items are safe to commit to a public repo; for the rest commit a link.

## Rush Hour & sliding blocks

- **hearn2005-sliding-block-ncl** — Hearn, R. A. & Demaine, E. D. (2005). PSPACE-completeness of sliding-block puzzles and other problems through the Nondeterministic Constraint Logic model of computation. *Theoretical Computer Science* 343(1–2). https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0205005 — arXiv. Paper. NCL gadgets give the cleanest picture of *why* Rush Hour levels are hard (AND/OR "latches"); useful vocabulary for designing dependency chains.
- **hearn2006-games-puzzles-computation-thesis** — Hearn, R. A. (2006). *Games, Puzzles, and Computation*. PhD thesis, MIT. https://erikdemaine.org/theses/bhearn.pdf — author copy (thesis). Paper. Book-length NCL treatment incl. Rush Hour, sliding blocks, bounded vs unbounded games; the reference for complexity of every variant the game might add.
- **tromp2005-limits-rush-hour-logic** — Tromp, J. & Cilibrasi, R. (2005). Limits of Rush Hour Logic complexity. arXiv cs/0502068. https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0502068 — arXiv. Paper. PSPACE-hard even with only 1×2 cars; also studies Unit (1×1) Rush Hour, relates it to mazes and gives empirical evidence of hardness — small, uniform pieces already suffice for deep puzzles.
- **brunner2020-1x1-rush-hour-fixed-blocks** — Brunner, J., Chung, L., Demaine, E. D., Hendrickson, D., Hesterberg, A., Suhl, A. & Zeff, A. (2020). 1×1 Rush Hour with fixed blocks is PSPACE-complete. FUN 2020. https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.09914 — arXiv. Paper. Even unit blocks with fixed walls are PSPACE-complete; relevant if the game ever uses 1-cell blocks or immovable obstacles.
- **fernau2003-parameterized-complexity-generalized-rush-hour** — Fernau, H., Hagerup, T., Nishimura, N., Ragde, P. & Reinhardt, K. (2003). On the parameterized complexity of a generalized Rush Hour puzzle. CCCG 2003. https://web.cs.dal.ca/~cccg/papers/22.pdf — author/proceedings copy (CCCG site). Paper. **Key for the infinite board**: polynomial algorithms when the number of vehicles or of moves is bounded — i.e. complexity is driven by car count, not board size; the "only finitely many relevant positions" idea is exactly what an endless board needs.
- **vanassema2014-hardness-6x6-rush-hour** — van Assema, J. (2014). On the hardness of 6×6 Rush Hour: an exploration of the entire configuration space. BSc thesis, Univ. of Amsterdam. https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/b.bredeweg/pdf/BSc/20132014/VanAssema.pdf — institution copy, copyright author. Paper. Full enumeration of the 6×6 space; distribution of hardness, re-check of Collette et al.
- **cian2022-modeling-solving-rush-hour** — Cian, L., Dreossi, T. & Dovier, A. (2022). Modeling and solving the Rush Hour puzzle. CILC 2022, CEUR-WS Vol-3204 paper 29. https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3204/paper_29.pdf — **CC-BY 4.0**. Paper. Same puzzle modelled in ASP, Prolog, PDDL planners and MiniZinc with timings; quick guide to which declarative/planner encodings scale and how to generalise board/exit.
- **bockholt2018** — *not downloaded* (see below).

## Difficulty & human modelling

- **olieslagers2024-backward-reasoning-and-or-trees-cogsci** — *not downloaded* (eScholarship blocks scripts; see below). The short CogSci version of olieslagers2026.
- **vanopheusden2023-expertise-planning-depth** — van Opheusden, B., Kuperwajs, I., Galbiati, G., Bnaya, Z., Li, Y. & Ma, W. J. (2023). Expertise increases planning depth in human gameplay. *Nature* 618, 1000–1005. https://www.cns.nyu.edu/malab/static/files/publications/2023_Opheusden,Kuperwajs,Galbiati,BnayaLi,Ma.pdf — author copy on lab site, © Springer Nature. Paper. Best-first-search player model with fitted planning depth, pruning and feature weights (Ma lab; same methods family as the Rush Hour work); a template for a "simulated human" difficulty estimator.
- **correa2023-humans-decompose-tasks** — Correa, C. G., Ho, M. K., Callaway, F., Daw, N. D. & Griffiths, T. L. (2023). Humans decompose tasks by trading off utility and computational cost. *PLoS Computational Biology* 19(6): e1011087. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011087 — **CC-BY**. Paper. Formal model of which subgoals people choose; fits "goals" as designed intermediate objectives.
- **ho2022-simplified-mental-representations** — Ho, M. K., Abel, D., Correa, C. G., Littman, M. L., Cohen, J. D. & Griffiths, T. L. (2022). People construct simplified mental representations to plan. *Nature* 606, 129–136. https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06948 — arXiv preprint. Paper. "Value-guided construal": players ignore obstacles that don't matter — suggests a difficulty term for how many blocks must be *attended to*, and for decoy blocks.
- **huys2012-bonsai-trees-pruning** — Huys, Q. J. M., Eshel, N., O'Nions, E., Sheridan, L., Dayan, P. & Roiser, J. P. (2012). Bonsai trees in your head: how the Pavlovian system sculpts goal-directed choices by pruning decision trees. *PLoS Comput Biol* 8(3): e1002410. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002410 — **CC-BY**. Paper. People prune branches that start with a locally bad-looking move — explains why levels needing a "step back first" feel hard.
- **jarusek2010-sokoban-difficulty-rating** — Jarušek, P. & Pelánek, R. (2010). Difficulty rating of Sokoban puzzle. STAIRS 2010, 140–150. https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/stairs2010-final.pdf — author copy, © IOS Press. Paper. Precursor of jarusek2011: decomposition metric + human-like state-space traversal model, validated on human solving times.
- **vankreveld2015-automated-puzzle-difficulty-estimation** — van Kreveld, M., Löffler, M. & Mutser, P. (2015). Automated puzzle difficulty estimation. IEEE CIG 2015, 415–422. https://ics-websites.science.uu.nl/docs/vakken/mscip/assignments/CIG2015-AutomatedPuzzleDifficultyEstimation.pdf — author institution copy, © IEEE. Paper. Cheap feature-based difficulty function fitted to player ratings (Flow, Lazors, Move) — a practical baseline next to search-based measures.
- **chu2025-what-makes-puzzle-fun** — Chu, J., Zheng, K. & Fan, J. E. (2025). What makes people think a puzzle is fun to solve? CogSci 2025. https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cognitivesciencesociety/article/49291/galley/37252/download — **CC-BY 4.0**. Paper. Separates *fun* from *difficulty* on Sokoban (corpus of 460 rated levels + experiment); the generator should optimise enjoyment, not just hardness. Code/data: `code/fun-puzzles_cogsci25`.
- **kristensen2021-statistical-modelling-level-difficulty** — Kristensen, J. T., Valdivia, A. & Burelli, P. (2021). Statistical modelling of level difficulty in puzzle games. IEEE CoG 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03305 — arXiv. Paper. Survival/attempt-count models of difficulty from live player telemetry; what to log once the game has players.
- **kristensen2020-completion-rate-reinforcement-learning** — Kristensen, J. T., Valdivia, A. & Burelli, P. (2020). Estimating player completion rate in mobile puzzle games using reinforcement learning. IEEE CoG 2020. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626 — arXiv. Paper. Agent's best-5% runs predict human completion rate; relative (not absolute) agent difficulty tracks humans.

## Generation

- **sturtevant2018-exhaustive-pcg** — Sturtevant, N. R. & Ota, M. J. (2018). Exhaustive and semi-exhaustive procedural content generation. AIIDE 2018, 109–115. https://www.cs.du.edu/~sturtevant/papers/sturtevant18epcg.pdf — author copy, © AAAI. Paper. Enumerate all levels, then filter/select (Fling!); the principled version of "generate on small windows exhaustively, then pick".
- **smith2010-expressive-range-level-generator** — Smith, G. & Whitehead, J. (2010). Analyzing the expressive range of a level generator. PCGames 2010. https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/smith2010analyzing.pdf — workshop archive copy, © ACM. Paper. Heat-map evaluation of a generator over two metrics (e.g. difficulty × solution shape) — how to check the goal generator isn't stuck in one style.
- **taylor2011-procedural-generation-sokoban** — Taylor, J. & Parberry, I. (2011). Procedural generation of Sokoban levels. GAMEON-NA 2011, 5–12. https://ianparberry.com/pubs/GAMEON-NA_METH_03.pdf — author copy, © EUROSIS. Paper. Template rooms + reverse search from goal to find the farthest start; the classic "generate backwards" recipe.

## State-space reduction

- **goubault2013-geometric-view-por** — Goubault, E., Heindel, T. & Mimram, S. (2013). A geometric view of partial order reduction. *ENTCS* 298, 179–195. https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/19274418/Goubalt_Heindel_ET_AL_2013_A_Geometric_View_of_Partial_Order_Reduction.pdf — publisher PDF via Edinburgh repository (ENTCS is open access, likely CC BY-NC-ND; verify before committing). Paper. Independent moves as cubes in a directed space: the "blocks moving independently form product regions" picture from the AI chat.
- **gnad2018-star-topology-decoupled-search** — Gnad, D. & Hoffmann, J. (2018). Star-topology decoupled state space search. *Artificial Intelligence* (2018). https://fai.cs.uni-saarland.de/hoffmann/papers/ai18.pdf — author preprint, © Elsevier. Paper. Search over the "center" while leaves (independent blocks) are tracked as sets; directly applicable when most blocks on a huge board don't interact.
- **gnad2019-strong-stubborn-sets-decoupled-search** — Gnad, D., Hoffmann, J. & Wehrle, M. (2019). Strong stubborn set pruning for star-topology decoupled state space search. *JAIR* 65, 343–392. https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11576 — JAIR open access (© AI Access Foundation). Paper. Journal version combining decoupling + stubborn sets (extends gnad2016 in hand).
- **wehrle2014-efficient-stubborn-sets** — Wehrle, M. & Helmert, M. (2014). Efficient stubborn sets: generalized algorithms and selection strategies. ICAPS 2014. https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/wehrle-helmert-icaps2014.pdf — author copy, © AAAI. Paper. How to compute stubborn sets cheaply; implementation guide for POR in a solver.
- **wehrle2012-por-planning-vs-verification** — Wehrle, M. & Helmert, M. (2012). About partial order reduction in planning and computer aided verification. ICAPS 2012. https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/wehrle-helmert-icaps2012.pdf — author copy, © AAAI. Paper. Maps planning POR to model-checking POR (stubborn/ample/persistent sets); good orientation.
- **alkhazraji2012-stubborn-sets-optimal-planning** — Alkhazraji, Y., Wehrle, M., Mattmüller, R. & Helmert, M. (2012). A stubborn set algorithm for optimal planning. ECAI 2012 (2-page short paper). https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/alkhazraji-et-al-ecai2012.pdf — author copy, © IOS Press. Paper. Original strong-stubborn-sets-for-optimal-A* result.
- **roger2020-atom-centric-stubborn-sets** — Röger, G., Helmert, M., Seipp, J. & Sievers, S. (2020). An atom-centric perspective on stubborn sets. SoCS 2020. https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/roeger-et-al-socs2020.pdf — author copy, © AAAI. Paper. The "atom-centric" variant now default in Fast Downward (see web/fast-downward-pruning); defines stubborn sets via facts (cells) rather than actions — natural for grid occupancy.
- **pochter2011-problem-symmetries-state-based-planners** — Pochter, N., Zohar, A. & Rosenschein, J. S. (2011). Exploiting problem symmetries in state-based planners. AAAI 2011, 1004–1009. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/8014 — © AAAI, free. Paper. Symmetry detection/pruning via graph automorphisms; for identical blocks and translation symmetry on an endless board.
- **felner2004-additive-pattern-databases** — Felner, A., Korf, R. E. & Hanan, S. (2004). Additive pattern database heuristics. *JAIR* 22, 279–318. https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.0050 — arXiv (JAIR open access). Paper. Static/dynamic additive PDBs on sliding-tile puzzles; follow-up to korf2002 in hand.
- **junghanns2001-sokoban-relevance-cuts** (`.ps`, not PDF) — Junghanns, A. & Schaeffer, J. (2001). Sokoban: improving the search with relevance cuts. *Theoretical Computer Science* 252(1–2), 151–175. Author preprint from Schaeffer's old UAlberta page via Wayback: https://web.archive.org/web/20080418201923/http://www.cs.ualberta.ca/~jonathan/Papers/Papers/tcs.ps — author copy, © Elsevier. Paper (PostScript; convert with `ps2pdf`/Ghostscript `-dSAFER` if wanted — PostScript is a program, so treat as untrusted). Move-influence pruning — a cheap, non-optimal locality cut for huge boards. `.pdf` converted from the `.ps` is in `papers/` too; checked 2026-11-11: same title, authors and abstract as the TCS article (DOI 10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00080-3), 26 pp., self-described "revised and enhanced version" of the CG'98 paper, i.e. the author preprint of the TCS paper.
- **seipp2020-saturated-cost-partitioning** — Seipp, J., Keller, T. & Helmert, M. (2020). Saturated cost partitioning for optimal classical planning. *JAIR* 67, 129–167. https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11673 (PDF https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/11673/26557/22832) — JAIR open access (© AI Access Foundation). Paper. How to add several admissible heuristics (PDBs, abstractions) without double counting by splitting operator costs; background for summing overlapping blocker-group PDBs (disjoint groups need no partitioning, see korf2002).
- **seipp2018-cegar-cartesian-abstraction** — Seipp, J. & Helmert, M. (2018). Counterexample-guided Cartesian abstraction refinement for classical planning. *JAIR* 62, 535–577. https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11217 (PDF https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/11217/26428/20797) — JAIR open access (© AI Access Foundation). Paper. CEGAR for planning heuristics: start coarse, split abstract states along a counterexample plan; the reference for the AI notes' "split intervals on a counterexample" idea for unbounded lanes. Also introduces saturated cost partitioning.
- **mazurkiewicz1977-concurrent-program-schemes-traces** — Mazurkiewicz, A. (1977). Concurrent program schemes and their interpretations. DAIMI Report Series PB-78, Aarhus University. https://doi.org/10.7146/dpb.v6i78.7691 (PDF https://tidsskrift.dk/daimipb/article/download/7691/6542) — CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 (DAIMI PB on tidsskrift.dk). Paper. The original trace-theory report (moves that commute are one "trace"); **scanned, no text layer — needs OCR to convert**. Readable modern intro, not downloaded: see Not downloaded.

## Infinite / large boards and big-search engineering

- **korf2004-best-first-frontier-search-ddd** — Korf, R. E. (2004). Best-first frontier search with delayed duplicate detection. AAAI 2004, 650–657. https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2004/AAAI04-103.pdf — © AAAI, free. Paper. Store only the frontier (no closed list) + disk-based duplicate detection; memory model for very large BFS. (Full JACM "Frontier search" paper: see Not downloaded.)
- **korf2005-large-scale-parallel-bfs** — Korf, R. E. & Schultze, P. (2005). Large-scale parallel breadth-first search. AAAI 2005. https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2005/AAAI05-219.pdf — © AAAI, free. Paper. Cited by delsolar2026 for exhaustive Rush Hour BFS; how to BFS spaces bigger than RAM.
- **zhou2004-structured-duplicate-detection** — Zhou, R. & Hansen, E. A. (2004). Structured duplicate detection in external-memory graph search. AAAI 2004. https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2004/AAAI04-108.pdf — © AAAI, free. Paper. Exploits locality (moves change only nearby state) to partition the space; matches block moves on a big board.
- **brumleve2012-infinite-chess-mate-in-n-decidable** — Brumleve, D., Hamkins, J. D. & Schlicht, P. (2012). The mate-in-n problem of infinite chess is decidable. arXiv 1201.5597 (CiE 2012). https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.5597 — arXiv. Paper. Decidability on an infinite board with finitely many pieces via "far away is equivalent" arguments — the template for proving/implementing bounded search on an endless board.
- **evans2014-transfinite-game-values-infinite-chess** — Evans, C. D. A. & Hamkins, J. D. (2014). Transfinite game values in infinite chess. arXiv 1302.4377 (later in *Integers*). https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4377 — arXiv. Paper. Shows what goes weird on infinite boards (unbounded but finite solution lengths); caution for "is this goal solvable at all" logic.
- **geller2022-baba-is-you-undecidable** — Geller, J. (2022). Baba is You is undecidable. arXiv 2205.00127. https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00127 — arXiv. Paper. Undecidability arises from extending a block-pushing level by an unbounded hallway — contrast case: which rule additions would make the endless game undecidable.
- **brunner2025-push-1-without-fixed-walls-pspace** — MIT Hardness Group (Brunner, J., Chung, L., Demaine, E. D., Diomidova, J., Hendrickson, D. & Lynch, J.) (2025). Pushing blocks without fixed walls via checkable gizmos: Push-1 is PSPACE-complete. arXiv 2508.19759. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19759 — arXiv. Paper. Hardness with only movable blocks and no walls — closest to an open, wall-less board.

- **code/downward** — Fast Downward planner, github.com/aibasel/downward, shallow clone at a744e44 (2026-11-09), 5.6 MB. GPL-3.0. Code. Reference implementations of stubborn sets (simple, EC, atom-centric), symmetry, PDBs, merge-and-shrink; see `src/search/pruning/`.

- **data/osf-23vrb** — OSF project 23vrb "A computational model of backward reasoning in human problem solving" (Olieslagers), https://osf.io/23vrb, copyright J. Olieslagers 2024 (no licence named on OSF). Data. Full listing in `listing.json` (521 files, 1.38 GB). Skipped (listed in `skipped.json`, 1.35 GB): `processed_data/stuff.jld2` (840 MB opaque Julia blob), `df_stats.csv`, `dict.jld2`, `mc_dict.jld2` (~85 MB each, also in the GitHub clone), `df_stats_TOL.jld2` and all Tower of London raw data (not Rush Hour).
- **code/rush** + **data/fogleman-rush** + **web/fogleman-rush** — Fogleman, M. (2018). Solving Rush Hour, the puzzle. https://www.michaelfogleman.com/rush/ ; github.com/fogleman/rush (Go/C++, MIT, 3e3b839, 612 KB). Code + data + web. Bitboard solver, enumerator, generator, clustering. - **code/fun-puzzles_cogsci25** — github.com/cogtoolslab/fun-puzzles_cogsci25 (53500c8, 56 MB). MIT. Code + data for chu2025: Sokoban corpus with like-rates (`data/study1/full_sokobanonline_df.csv`), experiment ratings, analysis notebooks.
- **code/sokoban-level-generator-mcts** — github.com/lkseg/sokoban-level-generator-mcts (d8a33d6, 14 MB). No licence (all rights reserved by default); contains a bundled `raylib.dll` binary — do not run. Code. C++ implementation of the kartal2016 MCTS generator with enhancements (cited in the AI chat).

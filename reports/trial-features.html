Trial features: which number tracks the verdicts

The twelve trial boards (8A–16B: reports/extra-moves.html; M1: extra-maker.html; G1, G2: extra-gen.html), each measured exactly on the board as played (its frame of walls), against how they felt. Made by test/experiments/trial-features.ts. Twelve boards and one player: every correlation here is a hint, not a finding. |ρ| needs about 0.58 to be "significant" at n = 12, and none of that holds with one player's mood and learning between sessions (the G trials came last).

The verdict order

Best/hardest first. Where the quotes say "hard" they also say "good" (12A, 16B, G2A), and the simple ones are the ones disliked, so one order serves both. The pairs are firm (the quotes compare them directly); the order between pairs is my reading and is soft in the middle:

  1. 12A — quite the challenge, lost track of blocks, enjoyed it; the best.
  2. 16B — more fun than 16A, harder to follow, but 12A was more difficult.
  3. M1A — much more difficult than M1B.
  4. G2A — better opening, slightly more difficult than G1A.
  5. G1A — not that difficult, one back-and-forth path.
  6. 16A — pretty easy, could follow the dependency chain.
  7. 8A — relatively simple, struggled once, liked slightly better than 8B.
  8. 8B — a bit easier than 8A.
  9. M1B — much easier than M1A (no other remark; placed by that).
  10. G1B — no outer blocks loose, slightly easier than G1A, worse than earlier trials.
  11. 12B — simple and straightforward, not good.
  12. G2B — the worst, no openings, very simple solution.

Pairs (the preferred side first): 8A > 8B, 12A > 12B, 16B > 16A, M1A > M1B, G1A > G1B, G2A > G2B. The pairs are matched at the same fewest moves, so "pairs right" is the cleaner test.

The numbers

Fewest
fewest moves, exact (breadth-first over every state of the blocks that matter).
Extra
extra moves, red included: moves − distinct blocks moved; the mean over every shortest solution (their range below it).
Extra w/o red
extra moves not counting red's own repeats (mean, range).
Extra share
extra moves / fewest (the Judge's share).
Red moves
times red moves (mean over shortest solutions).
Decisions
the Judge's decisions along the trial's proof (src/decisions.ts).
Judge difficulty
the Judge's difficulty: fewest + decision weight × decisions.
Judge balance
the Judge's balance (difficulty over distance, track blocks, × (1 + extra share)^4), as the game scores the goal.
Clearing depth
the deepest chain of blocks-to-clear in the Judge's clearing tree at the start.
Blocks moved
distinct blocks a shortest solution moves (mean).
Blocks that matter
blocks whose lanes reach red's, transitively (the rest can't change the solution).
States to fewest
states reachable within the fewest moves.
Reachable
every state the blocks that matter can reach.
Legal moves
legal moves (every block, every distance) at each step of the proof, averaged.
Movable blocks
blocks that can move at each step of the proof, averaged.
Optimal first
first moves that keep the fewest (lead to a shortest solution).
Optimal / step
moves that keep the fewest at each step of the proof, averaged (1 = forced all the way).
Forced steps
steps of the proof with exactly one move that keeps the fewest.
Shortest solutions
how many distinct shortest move sequences there are (log₁₀ for the correlation).
On a shortest path
share of the states within the fewest that lie on some shortest solution.
Longest chain
the longest run of moves each needing the one before (sharing a cell or a block), mean over shortest solutions.
Chain share
longest chain / fewest (1 = one single chain, low = many independent pieces).
Switches
interleaving: moves that don't depend on the move just before (a jump to another sub-chain), the least over shortest solutions.
Open threads
the most blocks at once that were moved and must move again (to keep in mind), the least over shortest solutions.
Return gap
the most other moves between two moves of one block, the least over shortest solutions.
Open edge
blocks touching the frame that can move at the start / blocks touching the frame.
Loose at start
blocks that can move at the start / all blocks (locked: can never move).
Played moves
the player's moves (log; 8A–16B were played before logging).
Played / fewest
the player's moves / fewest.
Time
seconds from the goal shown to reached (before the first move in brackets).
Solve time
seconds from the first move to reached.
Take-backs
undos, plus moves that put a block straight back where its last move started.

Exact on the framed board: blocks that can never move, or whose lanes never meet red's chain, are left out of the search (they can't change it). Per-solution numbers are over every shortest solution, or a uniform sample of 20,000 where there are more (G1A). Small grey numbers: the range over shortest solutions. 8A–16B have no play log (16A and 16B's files are empty reloads); no trial used undo.

What it says

  • The extra-moves family tracks the verdicts best, and only it does: extra moves (ρ 0.52), extra moves without red (0.55), open threads (0.55: the most blocks moved and still to move again), return gap (0.53) and longest chain (0.51). Each orders all four clear pairs right. On these boards they are nearly one number: every board with no extra moves has no open threads and no return gap, so they can't be told apart here. Open threads is the one that reads like the 12A quote ("lost track of which blocks were moved and which they wanted to move"). Reordering the soft middle of the verdict order moves their ρ between about 0.46 and 0.71. The other numbers stay below 0.4.
  • Extra moves alone holds up for the clear pairs, not the slight ones: 12A over 12B, 16B over 16A, M1A over M1B and G2A over G2B all have more extra moves on the preferred side. Both pairs the player called only slightly apart go the other way: 8A (0 extra) was liked a little better than 8B (3), and G1A (0) was rated above G1B (6; "worse than earlier trials"). In those two pairs the preferred side moves more distinct blocks and has far more shortest solutions and legal moves (8A 138 against 8 solutions, G1A 91,080 against 1,928), so more of the board is in play. In the clear pairs that goes the other way, and two cases can't separate it from noise.
  • Not tracking: fewest moves (ρ 0.22; the pairs are matched on it anyway), the Judge's decisions (−0.19 and 0 of 4 clear pairs: G2B, the worst, has 12; G1B has 11; 12A has 4), so the Judge's difficulty (−0.10) and its balance (0.31) are held up only by their extra-moves factor. Reachable states, states to the fewest, legal moves, movable blocks, the clearing tree's depth and the open edge as counted here (≈ 0) don't track either.
  • The play log (six boards): the time from the first move to the goal orders them best (ρ 0.66; M1A 48 s, G2A 31 s), but G2A against G2B is 31.3 s against 31.1 s, a tie. Moves over fewest and take-backs say little. G1A had 88 s of looking before the first move. No undo was used.
  • So, to generate: aim at real extra moves (over every shortest solution, not the proof's), read as blocks the player must come back to. Fewest moves and decisions don't buy the feel. A board with extra moves but little else in play (G1B, 8B) can still feel thin, but that rests on two slight verdicts.

Measurement notes. M1A and M1B are cut from the maker's world: 36 and 63 blocks matter there, too many to search exactly. M1A is searched exactly over the 19 blocks the proof moves or whose reach crosses a cell it sweeps, with the rest frozen. That confirms 13 moves on the cut, with 1 to 2 extra (the answer key says 2). M1B, even cut to the 12 blocks the proof moves, passed 2 million states before 12 moves, so its fewest is the Auditor's and only its proof is measured. The open edge counts blocks touching the trial's frame; on G1 and G2 the room's own edge is the ring of locked blocks inside the window, which this count doesn't see, so it doesn't reproduce "only 2 outer blocks open" on G1A.

Blocks on the track

Blocks lying the same way as the red block, in its own lane, between it and the far end of its target: it can only push them ahead of it, some past the target. The same 40 boards (densities 4 to 28, 8 boards each), the goal made by the old ranking and by the new one, which halves a candidate's score for each such block.

How many blocks were on the track

Goals by the number of blocks on their track, for goals made while you wait (level 0) and goals made ahead (level 2, most goals).

What it costs

Without those blocks to push, goals are easier and closer: most of the old goals' fewest moves were spent pushing them.

A bad and a good goal on the same board

Density , made ahead. Red: the red block; dashed: its target; orange: blocks on its track; blue: other blocks.

Conclusions

  • Old: 9 goals in 10 had blocks on the red block's track, most of them two or more.
  • New: almost none (1 in 40 while you wait, 0 of 40 made ahead): there is nearly always a goal without them.
  • The price: goals made ahead need about 11 fewest moves instead of 17, and lie about 10 cells away instead of 16. A new way to make goals hard without pushing blocks along the track would win that back.
  • They are also made in half the time.

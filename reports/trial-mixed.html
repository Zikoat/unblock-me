Mixed come-backs: does the order of the extra moves matter?

Extra moves (a block moved again, red included) tracked most blind verdicts but failed J1: J1A, with 6 extra moves, felt simple (16 moves in 18 s), while J1B, with none, felt a lot harder and better (30 moves in 53 s). J1A's 6 extra moves are one tidy wave: clear five blocks, move red, put them back almost mirrored. The favourite, 12A, moves red three times between its come-backs. The idea tested here: what feels hard is not how many come-backs there are, but how mixed they are. Made by test/experiments/trial-mixed.ts and trial-mixed-report.ts. Twenty boards and one player: every number here is a hint, not a finding.

The numbers

A trip is a block's way from one of its moves to its next: out, then back. Each number is taken over every shortest solution (or a uniform sample of 50,000 where there are more); the tables show its range, the correlations use its mean, except Mixed, which uses its least.

Extra moves
how many moves are come-backs: for each block, its moves less one, red included (the earlier measure).
Red moves
how often red moves.
Red in between
red's moves, before its last, made while some other block is still out and has to come back. 0: red moves once, at the end, or only when everything is back in place.
Past out-and-back
moves beyond a plain out-and-back: for each block (red too), its moves less two, if more than two. A block that is moved out, back, and out again counts 1.
Out at once
the most blocks (not red) moved and still to come back, at one time: how much to keep in mind.
Waves
separate rounds of clearing and putting back: how often the count of blocks still to come back rises from none.
Crossing trips
pairs of trips that cross. A trip is a block's way from one of its moves to its next (out, then back). Two trips cross when the second starts while the first is out and the first comes back while the second is still out: put back in a different order than taken out, A out, B out, A back, B back.
Nested trips
pairs of trips one inside the other, like brackets: A out, B out, B back, A back. The tidy kind of come-back.
Back out of order
come-backs made while a block moved later is still out: the stack-like order (last out, first back) broken.
Longest trip
the most come-back moves (of any block, red's too) between the two ends of one trip: how long a block stays out.
Mixed
the number picked: Red in between + Crossing trips, the least over every shortest solution (the simplest way through still has this much). 0 for one tidy wave, whatever its size.
Mixed share
Mixed / extra moves (0 with no extra moves): how much of the come-back work is mixed rather than tidy.
Fewest
the fewest moves (for comparison).

What it says

  • Mixedness does not beat plain extra moves on these boards. The number picked, Mixed (red's moves in between plus crossing trips, least over shortest solutions), has ρ +0.28 and orders 4 pairs right, 3 wrong and 2 tied; extra moves has ρ +0.30 and 5 right, 4 wrong. None of the mixedness numbers reaches ρ 0.45, and none follows solve time. Twenty boards can't tell these numbers apart.
  • Where it helps: it puts J1A low (Mixed 1, against 12A's 3 and G2A's 6) though J1A has as many extra moves as they do, which fits "one wave felt simple". Mixed is the pick because red moving while blocks are still out, and blocks put back in a different order, is what 12A ("lost track of which blocks I had moved") and G2A have and the tidy waves (J1A, 16B) mostly don't; it is simple to say and to count.
  • Where it can't help: J1B has no come-backs at all (0 on every number here), yet it was the harder one. No count of come-backs, mixed or not, can call it harder: what made J1B hard must be something else (it has 94,000+ shortest solutions against J1A's 4, so it's not a forced line; the player made 30 moves for 14). And G1B is as mixed as 12A but was liked less than G1A, which has none (a slight verdict). H2B over H2A is a tie on Mixed (both 0) where extra moves gets it right.
  • So: keep extra moves as the main number, and read Mixed as a second one that separates a tidy wave from interleaved come-backs at the same count. The K pairs below hold the count steady and vary only Mixed, which is the test this data can't give.

Pulling world blocks into a room

Your idea: take a finished open room (data/open-gen/, the H and J rooms' libraries) and slide world blocks into it, as a player could have, onto empty cells of the room; the puzzle then needs them moved out first. Made by test/experiments/open-pull.ts: every legal slide of a world block that ends on a room cell some shortest solution uses (elsewhere a pulled block is in no one's way), the block then counted as the room's, kept only if open-core.ts's two bounds still agree on the fewest and extra moves and on every mixedness number (so all of them hold in any surroundings); the best pull kept, then a second on top of it.

  • It added almost nothing. 34 rooms (3 not exact as made within the search's limit, left out): 1,168 pulls tried, each a world block slid onto a room cell some shortest solution uses, after at most one other world move to open its lane. 18 rooms had no such pull at all. 365 pulls added a move or mixedness on the upper bound alone (the room's blocks moving, the world frozen; 184 more too big to search), but only 2 of those were proved exactly: high6.jsonl:1, 16 → 17 moves and high6.jsonl:11, 14 → 15 moves, each one move more with the extra moves and mixedness unchanged (the pulled block is moved once, out of the way, so it never comes back). The rest failed the lower bound's limit (30 of the full checks went past 1.5 million states: a block reaching into the room from the world lets the lower bound try far more ways). A second pull on top never added anything.
  • Why so little: the cells a solution needs are inside the room, and the room's edge was opened only where that didn't change its goal: an edge cell next to a lane the solution needs stayed a wall (open-gen.html: "the walls left are the ones guarding the come-back lanes"). So a world block mostly reaches the ring's outer cells, which only some shortest solutions pass; a pulled block there closes those ways and another of the same length is left. And a pulled block is moved once, so it adds to the fewest, never to the come-backs. More from this would need the pull planned with the opening (a lane left open on purpose, its block pulled in onto a come-back lane), not added after it.
  • Where no pull added, the first exact one is kept anyway (8 rooms): the pulled block sits in the room and closes some ways, every number exact and the same.
  • Time: 213 s for the slowest room; 39 minutes for all of them on one core (this VM).

Generating real extra moves

Extra moves: Σ over the blocks a shortest solution moves of (times moved − 1), red counting, the least over every shortest solution (reports/extra-moves.html). The open, randomly filled world hardly holds goals with real ones (reports/extra-maker.html), so these experiments make boards that do, reusing nothing of the maker: an exact breadth-first solver for a bounded grid (test/experiments/extra-gen-core.ts) and two generators on it (extra-gen-make.ts). A board succeeds when its goal needs 10 to 16 moves and has 3 or more extra moves, both verified exactly; one that runs out of time falls back to today's random placement.

Next: players saw these rooms' edge as a closed ring of stuck blocks; reports/open-gen.html drops the seal and proves the goal exact in any surroundings instead, with the edge's blocks free to move.

The two approaches

  • A: rooms, then open them up. Random walled 6x6 rooms (10 to 14 blocks, up to 2 walls inside) or 7x7 (13 to 17, up to 4), each searched whole, which gives every goal's fewest at once; the best goal's extra moves over every shortest solution; until one has 3 or more. Either searched there and then ("A: rooms, searched now"), or drawn from a library made ahead (extra-moves.ts's 328 such goals; "from a library").
  • B: constructive placement. Red on an empty 6x6 or 7x7 region, its target at the far end of its lane; simulated annealing over adding, removing, moving and sliding blocks and adding or removing walls, scored by 3 × extra moves + the fewest (up to 16, less past it), each step solved exactly (only as deep as the goal, so early boards cost almost nothing).
  • Opening up (A, and B in "B, then opened up"): the room put into the world (2 cells of the world's own blocks and walls round its ring of walls, randomLayout at the game's density and 4% walls); then its walls taken away one at a time, each kept only if the goal's fewest stays the same and its extra moves don't drop: first two neighbouring walls swapped for one block lying along them (with walls or other stuck blocks at its ends it can never move: a wall that looks like a block), then each wall left removed or swapped for a block over it and an empty neighbour, then walls and blocks moved a cell out or in for a jagged edge; last, distractor blocks on empty cells no shortest solution passes through (they only add obstruction and no shortest solution meets them, so the fewest and extra moves can't change), near the cells the solutions use; then verified once more.
  • Exact, and sealed. The solver leaves out blocks that can never move (walls, and blocks locked against walls and each other along their lanes: they are walls to it) and blocks whose reach along their lane never meets one that matters; neither changes any solution. In the runs below the opening keeps the board sealed: no block that matters can reach the window's edge, nor can a block from past it slide in along a lane as far as one that matters, so the fewest and extra moves hold in the endless world whatever lies past the window. The trials are verified on their own board, the window framed by walls, which lets more of the edge open.

What works, and why

  • Neither generator is fast enough on the device alone. B (3% within 200 ms, 30% within 1 s, 80% within 5 s) and A searching rooms there and then (8%, 27%, 70%) both need about a second at the median on this VM. Both reach the same goals: 10 to 16 moves with 3 to 8 extra, about 4 on average. A pays for searching every room whole (10,000 to 150,000 states) before it knows its goals, and only one room in ten to twenty has such a goal; B aims at one goal, so each step searches only as deep as that goal and the early boards are tiny, and the annealing finds the cyclic dependencies itself (blocks that step aside and come back, red stepping back), but needs hundreds of steps.
  • Rooms made ahead are the answer for a phone: from a library, 100% succeed within 200 ms (median 240 ms, a little over the budget for the last check), and only the opening runs on the device. A library of a few hundred rooms (a few hundred bytes each; this one is extra-moves.ts's 328 goals, and either generator can grow it offline), varied by mirroring, rotating and the world round them. With 1 s the opening gets further (71% of the walls against 54%).
  • Opening up keeps the goal, and the world stays exact: about three in four of the room's walls go, nearly all swapped for blocks that are locked in place, and the board stays sealed. What doesn't open is a wall whose removal lets a lane of the room run out into the world: then a block from past the window could slide in, and the board can't be proved any more, or the state count runs away (the world's blocks all matter once a lane is open), or there is a shortcut (a few cases: 2 to 7 moves in place of 11). So the edge is mostly a ring of blocks stuck against each other and a few walls: not a square of walls, but a straight ring of blocks a careful player may notice. Moving it a cell out or in for a jagged edge almost always opens a lane, and is rejected when the board must stay sealed; on a framed trial board (G1, G2) more of the edge opens.
  • Distractors are cheap and free: on empty cells no shortest solution passes through, provably harmless; the rooms are already about as full as the world, so few fit (0 to 3, about 1 a board).

Wiring it into the world (not done)

  • Where the maker would place a goal, a pocket of the world (the room's cells, its ring and 2 cells round) is overridden: the generated blocks and walls replace the random ones there, as a fixed patch keyed to the chunks it covers and stored with the world, so every device sees the same board (like the island). The world's own blocks round it stay.
  • Generation: B in the goal worker with a budget of about 1 s, or rooms from a shipped library with only the opening on the device; fall back to today's maker when it runs out. Seeded, so the same seed gives the same patch.
  • The goal's fewest and proof come from the generator (exact while the patch stays sealed), not the measure; the Referee checks the proof as now, and the Judge would need to accept a proved fewest from the patch.
  • Open questions: blocks moved earlier near the patch (the patch must go on untouched ground, or be checked again against them); how far the ring of stuck blocks shows; and the ramp's short early goals, which B can aim at as easily.

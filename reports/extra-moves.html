Extra moves: trial pairs

Extra moves (cycle complexity): Σ over the blocks a solution moves of (times moved − 1), the moves less the distinct blocks moved; red counts. In a shortest solution a block never moves twice in a row, so each extra move is a block that has to come back after others moved. Each pair below has one goal with many extra moves and one with none or few, at the same fewest moves; which is which is in the answer key. Play each, note how it felt, then open the key.

Fewest: exact, by a breadth-first search of every state the board can reach. Extra (every shortest): the least and the most over every shortest solution (one number when all agree). Decisions: the Judge's, along the proof. In the pictures: red, its target dashed; blue blocks move once in the proof, yellow ones more often (the number is how many times), grey ones not at all.

  • Extra moves grow with the length: under 8 moves almost every goal has none or one, past 14 almost none does. A long goal with none is a long chain, each block in the way of the one before and every one moving once.
  • At the same length (10 to 14 moves) the board hardly tells them apart: goals with 0–1 extra and those with 4+ have about as many blocks (15.7 against 15.1), walls inside (2.1 against 2.0) and reachable states (median 26,594 and 21,193), and 21 of the 68 boards with a 4+ goal also hold a 0–1 one. It is the goal: which block, and where to. With many extra moves red moves more often (1.2 against 2.1 times), the busiest block more often (1.5 against 2.4), and the decisions are more (2.7 against 3.9): fewer blocks, taking turns, rather than many blocks each stepping aside once. So a maker that wants them can search goals on a board, not boards: score each candidate by its proof's extra moves.
  • Red itself is 28% of the extra moves in the proofs that have any (red going part way, then on once the way is cleared further).
  • Shortest solutions rarely differ in their extra moves: 218 of 6557 goals have shortest solutions with different counts, so the proof's count is nearly always what the player is held to.
  • The game's measure found no solution within its window and budget for 1268 of these goals, and a longer one than the fewest for 72; their proof here is a breadth-first one.

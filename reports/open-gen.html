Real extra moves in open rooms

The rooms of reports/extra-gen.html had real extra moves (Σ over the blocks a shortest solution moves of (times moved − 1), red counting) but were sealed: their edge was a ring of blocks locked in place, which players saw at once ("no outer blocks loose"). Here the room is no longer sealed. Its goal is verified exactly for any surroundings by two bounds that must agree, so the blocks on its edge and the world's round it may move; and the edge is measured as the player sees it.

Exact without a seal: two bounds

  • Upper bound: the room's blocks (R) searched exactly, every other block frozen where it is and the board's edge a wall. Each solution it finds moves only R's blocks inside the room, so it is legal wherever the room stands, whatever happens round it: the real fewest is at most this, and these extra moves are reachable.
  • Lower bound: R searched exactly while the world gives way. A world block that could ever move (it has a free cell, the board's edge, one of R's cells or another such block at an end of its lane) is soft: an R block may slide through it at one move more, after which it is gone for good. A world block that can never move is a wall; past the board every cell is free and R's blocks may slide out and back. Any real sequence of moves maps onto this one with no more moves: R's moves stay; when one of them passes a cell a world block had, that block must have moved at least once before (paid here as the toll, once); every other move of the world's is dropped. Where a real cell is free, the cell here is free or soft, so every R move stays legal. So the real fewest is at least this one's.
  • Agreeing on the fewest and on the least and most extra moves over every shortest solution, they are exact in the endless world and on any board round the room (the trial board's frame included): a real shortest solution then maps onto a lower-bound solution of the same length, so it can move a world block only once (1 move, 0 extra), and its extra moves are R's, which both bounds pin. Unit-checked against a plain exact search on 330 small boards: the bounds never cross the truth, and in all 243 cases where they agree they equal it.
  • Why this answers "blocks beyond some ring can't matter": the world is never searched; each of its blocks costs at least one move to get out of the way, and that is all the proof needs. How far the world reaches doesn't matter, so no margin is chosen: the trial boards show 3 cells of world round the room only so the edge looks as it will in the world. The search stays the room's size (states below). Both searches also prune any state that can't finish within the upper bound (red's move plus one for every block on red's way: never more than the moves left), which keeps every state on a solution that short at its true depth.

What it takes

  • A room alone does not survive an open edge. With nothing but the lower bound's free world round it, the 13 to 16-move library rooms fall to 2 to 5 moves (their blocks leave through the edge); the old trial rooms G1B and G2A, exact on their framed trial boards, are 7 and 5 moves in the open world (G1A and G2B, with no extra moves, stay exact). Annealing a room from empty against the lower bound (as extra-gen's B, 60 to 150 s a room) never got past 4 moves, nor did annealing a ring of blocks round a library room.
  • Opening a walled room up does (makeOpenUp, open-gen-make.ts): a library room (no walls inside, 10 to 16 moves, 3+ extra: 9 kinds here) with a ring of walls round it, in the world (3 cells of it round, the game's density and 4% walls); each ring wall in turn, in a random order, removed or swapped for a block that sticks into the room, along the ring or out into the world, whichever leaves more blocks free to move, each kept only if the two bounds still agree at the room's fewest and 3+ extra moves. Every step is a full check of both bounds (1.7 s at the median, 304,111 states for the lower bound on average).
  • Why some walls stay. A block that must step aside and come back (an extra move) could, with an opening in its lane, leave through it instead for the same count: the fewest stays, but a shortest solution with fewer extra moves appears. So the walls that stay are the ones capping the lanes of the blocks that move twice; everywhere else the edge opens.

The edge now

  • Its blocks move: on the room's edge 53% of the blocks can move at the start (old G rooms: 4%), in the ring just outside 86% (old: 40%; the world's own blocks: 70%).
  • But it keeps walls, more than before: 8.9 of the room edge's 28 cells on average (7 to 13), where the old rooms had 6.8 and a ring of stuck blocks for the rest. The old opening turned walls into blocks locked in place; this one only keeps a block that can move, and where none will do, the wall stays. Those are the walls capping the lanes of the blocks that move twice (above): the stuck ring is gone, but the room shows as a square with a wall about every third cell of its edge, the world's blocks moving freely round it.
  • Fewer blocks on the edge: 6.9 blocks touch it (old: 12.3): a wall taken away mostly stays an empty cell, which opens a neighbour.

6 and more extra moves (J trials)

The H rooms had 3 to 4 extra moves, and their pairs felt only a little different (H2 and H3 solved in under 15 s); the earlier boards with 6 to 7 (12A, G2A, M1A) felt clearly different. The library holds few rooms with 6+ extra at 12 to 16 moves and no walls inside (8 goals), so these rooms are climbed first, then opened up exactly as above.

  • Climbing rooms (open-high.ts): 20 climbs of 60 s each, two at once (10 minutes of the VM's two cores), each from a high-extra goal of the library (its walls taken out), extra-gen's annealed rooms or a random room, annealed on the exact solver of the sealed room (blocks added, removed, moved and slid, the target nudged), scored by the least extra moves over every shortest solution. 17 of 20 climbs reached 6+ extra at 12 to 16 moves without walls inside, 8,469 distinct rooms in all (1,936 with 6, 2,970 with 7, 2,594 with 8, 969 with 9); the first in a climb after 0.903 s at the median. Rooms are no longer the scarce part.
  • Opening them (open-gen.ts --lib, makeOpenUp with 6+ extra and the room's fewest kept): two rooms from each climb, its most extra and its least, 14 opened, 300 s each, two at once. All keep their fewest and 6+ extra exactly in any surroundings (the bounds must agree at every step); the walls left are the ones guarding the come-back lanes.
